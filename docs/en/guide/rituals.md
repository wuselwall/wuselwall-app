# Rituals

Chores say what needs doing. Rituals say who we are: small moments that come back and that the family knows. WuselWall has a few of them: **birthdays**, **looking forward**, **family traditions**, **meal wishes**, the **question at the table** and the **look back at our week**.

For all of them:

- **A ritual is never a chore.** It earns no points, keeps no streak and cannot be "missed". If you skip it, nothing is left behind.
- **Nobody is compared.** Nothing counts one person against another.
- **No notifications.** Rituals show up where you look anyway: on the wall, on **Today** and on the children's **My day**.
- **Everything can be switched off**, each one on its own, under **Settings → Household settings → Rituals** (see [Household settings](/en/settings/household#rituals)). At the start everything is on.

## Birthdays {#birthdays}

You enter a birthday on the person's page: **Settings → Members**, tap the person, **Birthday → Choose a date** (see [Edit a member](/en/settings/members-devices#edit-a-member)). Everyone can enter their own; guardians enter everyone else's.

Before the day it is counted down (see [Looking forward](#countdowns)), with the age the person turns: "Emma turns 9 · 3 more sleeps". On the wall the birthday stands under the date of its day all week, with a cake: "Emma turns 9". On the day, bunting in the person's colour hangs over the wall, Wusel wears the party hat, **Today** shows a birthday card, and on the kids' tablet Wusel cheers for the birthday child. With the occasion **Birthdays** switched off under **Seasons and occasions** (see [Wusel and colours](/en/guide/wusel#occasions)), none of this happens; the countdown stays.

Grandparents and friends stay yearly events, as before; you can mark them for looking forward.

In a [subscription to the family calendar](/en/calendar/feeds) each birthday is a yearly all-day event, without the age. When someone is **No longer with us**, their birthday is deleted.

## Looking forward {#countdowns}

"How many more sleeps?" The wall answers before anyone asks. There is a countdown for:

- every person's **birthday**
- the **first day of the school holidays**
- an **adult coming home** from a trip of two nights or more ("David comes home")
- any event with **We are looking forward to it** switched on in the editor, such as the trip to the zoo (see [Calendar](/en/calendar/#creating-an-appointment))

You see it on the **wall** in the footer, as many as fit; on **My day** under **Looking forward to** (the child's own countdowns and the whole family's); and on **Today** as one line with the nearest countdown.

Children and the wall count in nights up to a week ahead ("3 more sleeps"), and in days before that ("9 more days"). Adults read "in 3 days". The day before says **Tomorrow!**, the day itself **Today!**, and Wusel waves at the top of the wall. Then the countdown is gone.

How many days ahead it starts is set by **Start looking forward** (default 14 days, 7 to 60). **Count down to a parent's return** (default on) switches off the countdown for trips.

## Family traditions {#traditions}

Game night, pizza Friday, Sunday breakfast: whatever your family does again and again is easiest to start in the [calendar](/en/calendar/#new-tradition) with **New tradition**. You choose from **Game night**, **Pizza Friday**, **Film night**, **Sunday breakfast**, **Story time** and **Day out**, or **Your own tradition**. WuselWall fills in the editor: every week, the whole family, with a picture, and for Pizza Friday pizza for dinner. You change the day and time there before you save. Any repeating event also becomes a tradition when you switch on **Family tradition** in the editor. A tradition is **always on the wall**, even without **Show on the kitchen display**, with a lemon-yellow ribbon and its **picture**. Under **With a meal** you note which meal belongs to it, and whether it is lunch or dinner. When that meal is still free on the tradition's day, the [meal plan](/en/meals/) suggests it; one tap plans it. It never lands on the plan by itself.

On a tradition's day Wusel says in the morning on the wall, for example, "It's Game night today!".

If a tradition doesn't happen one week, delete **Only this event**. Nothing remembers that it was skipped.

## Meal wishes {#meal-wishes}

Each child may wish for a meal; you decide how many wishes may be open at once (one by default). That way the children have a say in the meal plan.

- **On My day** there is the card **Your meal wish**. **Wish** opens the choice: first **Family favourites**, the meals you have cooked most often lately, then **Our recipes**, each with a picture (with many recipes, **Find a recipe** helps), and at the bottom **Something else** with a few words, such as "pancakes", and a picture to go with them (**Pick a picture**). With **Wish for this** the wish is on its way. Until it is planned it can be **changed**.
- **The grown-ups** see the wishes at the top of the [meal plan](/en/meals/#wishes) under **Wishes**, each with its picture, and as a quiet card on **Today**. Tap a wish, pick a day and a meal, and it is planned.
- **When everyone may wish**, the grown-ups wish for themselves in the meal plan: **Make a wish** in the **Wishes** row opens the same choice, and **Change my wish** changes the open one.
- **Once planned**, the meal carries a star and "Jack's wish" in the plan and on the wall. The child's card then says, for example, "Your wish Pancakes is on Thursday!".

<Shot name="kid-wish" alt="My day on the kids' tablet with the card Your meal wish" wide />

A wish is **never turned down** in the app. A wish that isn't planned simply keeps waiting, the next week marked **from last week**. After four weeks a wish that was never planned quietly leaves the list and no longer counts; the child can simply wish for something new. Guardians can remove a wish; why is something you talk about together.

Who may wish is set by **Meal wishes**: **Children** (default), **Everyone** or **Off**. **Open wishes per person** allows up to three at once (default 1).

## Question at the table {#table-question}

A question for the table, answered aloud. Nothing is recorded: not who answered, not what, not even whether the question was asked.

The question appears during the meal: at the top of the wall's header, on **My day** and on **Today**. Every device shows the same question, and none comes back before all the others have had their turn.

- **Another question** shows the next one, but only on that device. Just say "take the next one".
- For a riddle, **Show the answer** reveals it.
- **Our questions** (on the question's card on **Today**): with **Add a question** anyone writes their own. They show "Question from Olivia". An own question is removed by whoever wrote it or by a guardian.
- A bundled question that doesn't suit you is taken out by a guardian with **Don't ask this one again**. Under **Questions we left out**, **Ask again** brings it back.

Settings: **Question at the table** applies to the **Warm meal** (default), to **Lunch and dinner**, or is **Off**. **Question at lunch** and **Question at dinner** set the times; no meal has to be planned for it. The default follows your region's country: 11:30 to 13:30 and 17:30 to 19:30, in France 19:00 to 21:00 for dinner, in Spain 13:30 to 15:30 and 20:00 to 22:00. **Questions for**: **All ages** works for a five-year-old, **Older children too** adds deeper questions for 8 and over.

::: info Languages
Each device shows the questions in its own language: about 150 in German and English, 50 in French and Spanish for now.
:::

## Our week {#week-review}

On Sunday you look back together at **Our week**: what *we* did. From Sunday 16:00 until Monday 10:00 there is a card on **Today** and **My day**, and a tile in the wall's footer. A tap opens a story of up to five cards, one at a time, filling the screen:

- **On** with a tap on the right, a swipe to the left or the arrow key; **back** with a tap on the left third, a swipe to the right or the other arrow key. The arrows at the bottom do the same.
- The bars at the top show how many cards there are and where you are. After the last card, with **Close** or with Esc, the look back closes.
- A card with nothing in it is left out, such as **Our traditions** in a week without one. With "reduce motion" on the device, the next card appears without sliding. Screen readers say "Card 2 of 4".

1. **What we did together**: the whole family's chores done, as one number
2. **Everyone had a moment**: one line for each person, such as a new badge, a day with everything ticked off, "was there for Games night" for a tradition or a key event, or simply "was part of our week". Nobody gets two, and nothing is a number compared with someone else's.
3. **Yum**: the week's meals, wishes with a star, new recipes marked "new"
4. **Our traditions**: the traditions that happened
5. **Next week we look forward to**: the nearest countdowns

Points, chores not done or a broken streak never appear in the look back. When it appears is set by **Our week**: a weekday and a time, or off. It stays until 10:00 the next morning. On the wall it returns to the week by itself after a while, like every detail.
